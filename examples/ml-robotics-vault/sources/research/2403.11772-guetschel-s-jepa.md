## TLDR

An exploratory study applying [[method:jepa]] (as "Signal-JEPA") to EEG
representation learning, aimed at seamless cross-dataset transfer for
brain-computer interfaces, introducing a domain-specific spatial
block-masking strategy and three new downstream classification
architectures, evaluated across three BCI paradigms on a 54-subject
dataset [[raw:2403.11772#ABSTRACT]].

## Contributions

- Introduces Signal-JEPA, including a novel domain-specific *spatial*
  block masking strategy for EEG (masking across channels, not just time)
  [[raw:2403.11772#ABSTRACT]].
- Proposes three novel downstream classification architectures built on
  the pretrained representations [[raw:2403.11772#ABSTRACT]].
- Evaluates across three distinct BCI paradigms — motor imagery, ERP, and
  SSVEP — providing preliminary evidence that spatial filtering matters
  more than mask size for downstream classification accuracy, and that
  pretraining-example length (but not mask size) influences downstream
  performance [[raw:2403.11772#ABSTRACT]].

## Method summary

The framework takes EEG recordings with $C$ channels and $T$ time samples
plus binary masks of length $L$. A Local encoder independently transforms
$t$ windows from each channel into $C \times t = L$ token embeddings of
dimensionality $d$; tokens are tagged by originating channel and temporal
position and flattened into a length-$L$ sequence
[[raw:2403.11772#INTRODUCTION]]. Only unmasked tokens go to the Contextual
encoder, while the full token sequence goes to a Contextual *target*
encoder that generates training targets; the Predictor then reconstructs
the masked tokens' representations, compared against the targets with an
$L_1$ loss [[raw:2403.11772#INTRODUCTION]]. The Contextual target
encoder's parameters are not trained by gradient descent — they follow the
Contextual encoder's parameters via Exponential Moving Average (EMA), the
same target-encoder pattern used across every JEPA variant in this vault
[[raw:2403.11772#INTRODUCTION]].
