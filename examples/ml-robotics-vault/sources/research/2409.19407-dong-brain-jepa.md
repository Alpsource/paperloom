## TLDR

Brain-JEPA is a brain-dynamics foundation model built on [[method:jepa]],
adapted to fMRI via two new techniques — Brain Gradient Positioning and
Spatiotemporal Masking — achieving state-of-the-art results on demographic
prediction, disease diagnosis/prognosis, and trait prediction, with
stronger off-the-shelf (linear-probing) performance and cross-ethnic-group
generalizability than the prior largest brain-activity model
[[raw:2409.19407#Abstract]].

## Contributions

- Brain Gradient Positioning: a functional coordinate system for brain
  functional parcellation used as positional encoding for different
  Regions of Interest (ROIs), replacing prior anatomical-position-only
  encodings that miss the fact nearby anatomical ROIs can have very
  different functional activation patterns
  [[raw:2409.19407#Abstract]] [[raw:2409.19407#1-Introduction]].
- Spatiotemporal Masking: a masking strategy tailored to fMRI's
  heterogeneous, spatiotemporally sparse time-series patches, addressing
  why directly reusing image-style JEPA masking is suboptimal for fMRI
  [[raw:2409.19407#Abstract]].
- State-of-the-art fine-tuned performance on demographic prediction,
  disease diagnosis/prognosis, and trait prediction, plus strong
  off-the-shelf linear-probing results and better generalization across
  ethnic groups than BrainLM, the prior representative fMRI foundation
  model [[raw:2409.19407#Abstract]].

## Method summary

A Vision Transformer observation encoder $f_\theta$ processes a single
observation (context) block and predicts the representations of separate
target blocks [[raw:2409.19407#1-Introduction]]. Spatiotemporal Masking
divides the non-observed input into three regions — Cross-ROI ($\alpha$),
Cross-Time ($\beta$), and Double-Cross ($\gamma$) — and samples target
blocks from each region separately, rather than masking uniformly at
random as in image JEPAs [[raw:2409.19407#1-Introduction]]. A narrower ViT
predictor $g_\phi$ takes the observation encoder's output and, conditioned
on positional embeddings (Brain Gradient Positioning for ROI location,
sine/cosine encoding for temporal position), predicts each target block's
representation; predictions are aligned against a target encoder
$f_{\bar\theta}$ whose weights are an EMA of the observation encoder's
weights [[raw:2409.19407#1-Introduction]] — the same EMA target-encoder
pattern as [[paper:2403.11772]] S-JEPA.

The paper's motivation for departing from a direct I-JEPA port is explicit:
fMRI has no natural token "order" the way image patches or sentence words
do, so anatomical-position encoding (as used by prior work BrainLM)
doesn't capture functional parcellation; and fMRI's smaller, sparser
datasets (versus e.g. ImageNet) need a stronger inductive bias than
I-JEPA's random multi-block context/target selection provides
[[raw:2409.19407#1-Introduction]].
